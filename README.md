# Medical-Project: Retrospective Data on Risk Certification for Babies

## Overview

This project provides comprehensive documentation on infant risk assessment, high-risk newborn identification, and AI-assisted risk certification using retrospective health data in maternity care.

## Purpose

The documentation covers:

- **Infant Risk Factors**: Prenatal, perinatal, and postnatal risk factors
- **High-Risk Newborn Assessment**: Multi-domain clinical approaches
- **Developmental Screening**: International comparison and best practices
- **AI-Based Risk Analysis**: Machine learning applications in infant health
- **Data Protection**: Safeguarding sensitive child health information

## Documentation

📖 **[Read the Complete Infant Risk Assessment Guide](infant-risk-assessment-guide.md)**

## Key Topics

1. **Risk Assessment Frameworks**: Understanding infant risk factors and assessment methods
2. **International Best Practices**: Developmental screening schedules across countries (Singapore, US, UK, Australia, Japan)
3. **AI Integration**: Machine learning and deep learning applications in health risk prediction
4. **Financial Risk Management (FRM)**: Relevance of FRM in AI-supported risk analysis
5. **Data Security**: Protecting babies' retrospective health data

## Context

This documentation supports healthcare professionals, risk analysts, and policymakers in understanding:

- How to identify and assess high-risk infants
- The role of AI in retrospective data analysis for risk certification
- Best practices in developmental screening and early intervention
- Data protection requirements for sensitive infant health information

## Target Audience

- Healthcare professionals in maternity and neonatal care
- Risk analysts working with health data
- Policy makers in child health and development
- Researchers in pediatric health outcomes
- Data scientists developing AI models for health risk prediction

---

**Note**: This documentation emphasizes that AI systems support risk certification and population-level analysis, not replace clinicians. Human oversight, data governance, and ethical safeguards remain essential.
