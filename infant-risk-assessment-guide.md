## 1. Infant Risk Factors

### 1️⃣ What are risk factors for infants?

Infant risk factors are conditions or exposures that **increase the likelihood of illness, developmental delay, disability, or mortality** during the neonatal and early infancy period.

### Key categories of infant risk factors

**A. Prenatal (before birth)**

* Maternal health conditions (e.g. diabetes, hypertension, infections)
* Poor maternal nutrition
* Substance exposure (alcohol, drugs, smoking)
* Inadequate antenatal care
* Short or long inter-pregnancy interval (IPI)

**B. Perinatal (around birth)**

* Preterm birth (<37 weeks gestation)
* Low birth weight (<2.5 kg)
* Birth complications (asphyxia, difficult delivery)
* Congenital anomalies
* Neonatal infections

**C. Postnatal (after birth)**

* Feeding difficulties
* Poor weight gain
* Infections
* Unsafe sleep environment
* Limited caregiver support
* Socioeconomic stressors

## 2. High-Risk Newborn Assessment

### 2️⃣ How do you assess a high-risk newborn?

A high-risk newborn is assessed using a **structured, multi-domain clinical approach**, not a single test.

### Core assessment domains

**1. Birth history**

* Gestational age
* Birth weight
* Mode of delivery
* Apgar scores
* Resuscitation required

**2. Medical condition**

* Respiratory stability
* Cardiac function
* Neurological status
* Infection risk
* Congenital abnormalities

**3. Maternal & family factors**

* Maternal medical history
* Pregnancy complications
* Social support and caregiving capacity

**4. Early neonatal course**

* Feeding tolerance
* Thermoregulation
* Blood glucose stability
* Jaundice levels

👉 Infants identified as high risk receive **closer monitoring, earlier follow-up, and multidisciplinary care**.

## 3. High-Risk Baby Identification

### 3️⃣ Who are considered to be high-risk babies?

High-risk babies are infants who have **one or more biological, medical, or social risk factors** that increase vulnerability.

### Common groups considered high risk

* **Preterm infants**
* **Low birth weight infants**
* **Babies with congenital anomalies**
* **Infants with birth asphyxia**
* **Infants with neonatal infections**
* **Babies born to mothers with significant medical conditions**
* **Infants from environments with limited caregiving or healthcare access**

High risk does **not** mean poor outcome—it means **higher monitoring needs**.

## 4. At-Risk Infant Definition

### 4️⃣ What is the definition of an "at-risk infant"?

An **at-risk infant** is:

> *An infant who, due to biological, medical, or environmental factors, has an increased probability of adverse health, developmental, or survival outcomes compared to the general infant population.*

Key points:

* Risk can be **temporary or persistent**
* Risk can be **medical, developmental, or social**
* Risk status requires **ongoing reassessment**, not a permanent label

## 5. Singapore's Low Infant Mortality Rate

### 5️⃣ Why is Singapore's infant mortality rate so low?

Singapore's low infant mortality rate is the result of **system-level strengths**, not a single intervention.

### Major contributing factors

**1. Strong antenatal care**

* Early and regular pregnancy monitoring
* Early detection of maternal and fetal risks

**2. High-quality perinatal and neonatal care**

* Skilled birth attendants
* Advanced neonatal intensive care units (NICUs)
* Rapid response to complications

**3. Universal childhood healthcare access**

* Structured well-child visits
* Integrated immunisation and developmental screening
* Early referral pathways

**4. Robust public health systems**

* High vaccination coverage
* Strong infection control
* Safe water, sanitation, and nutrition

**5. Effective risk stratification**

* Early identification of high-risk infants
* Targeted follow-up and intervention
* Continuity of care from birth through early childhood

**6. Socioeconomic stability**

* High maternal education levels
* Strong healthcare financing
* Low barriers to accessing care

## 7. AI-Based Risk Screening

### 2️⃣ Mapping to AI-based risk-screening touchpoints

These developmental screening visits align naturally with **AI-supported risk analysis** because they occur at **biologically meaningful risk windows**:

| Age Window                       | Clinical Purpose                   | AI-Based Risk Screening Role                                                   |
| -------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Neonatal (0–1 month)**         | Baseline health, feeding, reflexes | Establish baseline risk profile; detect early growth or neurological anomalies |
| **Early infancy (3–6 months)**   | Motor development, responsiveness  | Pattern detection for delayed motor or sensory development                     |
| **Late infancy (9–12 months)**   | Mobility, early communication      | Predictive modeling for developmental delay risks                             |
| **Toddler stage (18–30 months)** | Language & social development      | High-value window for ASD, speech, and behavioral risk modeling              |
| **Preschool (3–4 years)**        | Cognitive & social readiness       | Risk stratification for learning and behavioral outcomes                      |

### 3️⃣ Alignment with retrospective data & risk-certification workflows

In a **retrospective risk-certification workflow**, these touchpoints function as **data anchors** rather than real-time decision points.

**Workflow alignment:**

1. **Data capture**
   Developmental screening results (milestones, clinician notes, parental inputs) are recorded at each scheduled visit.

2. **Retrospective dataset construction**
   Longitudinal data across multiple touchpoints is compiled (e.g. infancy → toddler → preschool).

3. **AI-assisted risk analysis**
   Models analyze historical patterns across timepoints to identify:

   * Delayed or atypical developmental trajectories
   * Accumulated risk factors rather than single-visit anomalies

4. **Risk certification / stratification**
   Outputs support:

   * Population-level risk certification
   * Program eligibility
   * Policy-level planning and intervention prioritization
     *(Not autonomous clinical diagnosis)*

### 4️⃣ Policy & ethics-review–friendly summary (simplified language)

> Most healthcare systems conduct **multiple developmental screening or review visits** for infants and young children at defined ages. These visits generate structured developmental data across time.
>
> When used retrospectively, this data can support **AI-assisted risk screening** by identifying developmental patterns and risk trajectories across early life stages.
>
> AI systems in this context are designed to **support risk certification and population-level analysis**, not to replace clinicians. Human oversight, data governance, and ethical safeguards remain essential, particularly when handling sensitive child health data.

### ✅ Key takeaway

* Developmental screening schedules already provide **natural, age-based AI risk-screening touchpoints**
* Retrospective analysis strengthens risk accuracy by using **multiple timepoints**, not isolated checks
* AI supports **risk certification and policy planning**, with **human governance as a core requirement**

## 8. FRM and AI Relevance

### Will FRM be relevant, supported by AI?

**Yes, the FRM will be relevant.** The Financial Risk Manager (FRM) certification remains a globally respected benchmark for comprehensive risk management expertise. Its relevance is not diminished by advances in artificial intelligence (AI); rather, it is **strengthened by the integration of AI, Machine Learning (ML), and Deep Learning (DL)** into modern risk practices.

Recent GARP surveys indicate that **55% of financial risk managers now spend at least half of their time on core risk work**, while **42% identify AI and machine learning as key areas for continuing education**. These findings highlight both sustained demand for FRM-certified professionals and the profession's increasing focus on emerging domains such as climate risk, AI governance, and model risk management.

### AI, ML, and DL in Risk Management

AI, ML, and DL are reshaping Financial Risk Management by enabling **predictive analytics, real-time monitoring, and advanced pattern recognition**, allowing risk functions to move beyond static, rule-based models toward adaptive and forward-looking approaches.

Machine learning techniques identify complex relationships in structured financial data such as credit histories and transaction records, while deep learning models process large-scale and unstructured information, including reports and market signals, to support more nuanced risk modeling.

### Integrated Data Ecosystem

In practice, AI-enabled FRM operates within an integrated data ecosystem spanning the full data lifecycle:

* **SQL databases** securely store and process structured financial and transactional data, where aggregation, data quality checks, and risk metric calculations are performed
* **Business intelligence tools** (Power BI, Tableau) transform processed data into interactive dashboards for risk visualization, performance tracking, and decision support
* **Monitoring platforms** (Splunk) provide continuous, real-time monitoring of system logs and events to detect operational anomalies, cybersecurity threats, and compliance issues

### Human Expertise Remains Essential

Despite these technological advances, **human expertise remains essential**. As noted by the CFA Institute, professionals who embrace AI will replace those who ignore it, rather than AI replacing risk professionals outright.

AI models still require rigorous oversight to address issues of data quality, bias, explainability, validation, and governance—core responsibilities of risk managers. Consequently, AI does not reduce the value of the FRM designation; instead, **AI-literate FRM professionals who can manage both traditional and algorithmic risks are uniquely positioned to lead in the evolving financial landscape**.

## 9. Health Risk Prediction Using Retrospective Data

### What is health risk prediction related to the babies' retrospective data on risk certification?

The health risk prediction uses **machine learning algorithms trained on retrospective (historical) data** to identify patterns and risk factors associated with adverse outcomes in babies, such as preterm birth, sepsis, and congenital defects.

The **"risk certification"** refers to the process where these models assign a probability or a risk score to an individual based on their specific historical data.

### How is the babies' retrospective data on risk certification in maternity care?

Retrospective data on babies' risk certification in maternity care focuses on **identifying vulnerable infants likely to experience adverse health outcomes or requiring immediate protective interventions**. This data is increasingly used to validate risk stratification models and standardize care.

## 10. AI in Risk Management for Maternity Care

### Can risk analysts analyse using AI?

**Yes, risk analysts can analyse risks using AI.** In the Singapore context, this typically involves leveraging **AI Singapore (AISG) frameworks**, research outputs, and governance-aligned methodologies rather than relying on a single standalone software tool.

Analysts commonly use development environments such as **Visual Studio Code** to implement AISG-aligned AI and machine learning pipelines, enabling a shift from reactive, rule-based analysis toward proactive, predictive risk assessment.

### AI in Infant Risk Certification

In the area of infant risk certification, retrospective health data is increasingly analyzed using AI and machine learning models to:

* Identify potential health risks
* Support clinical screening
* Inform certification decisions

These models are developed and applied within established governance and ethical frameworks to ensure responsible use, transparency, and accountability while improving the accuracy and timeliness of risk analysis.

### Integrated Analytics Toolchain

This work is supported by an integrated analytics toolchain:

* **SQL Server / MySQL databases** for secure storage, processing, and management of retrospective infant health data
* **Visual Studio Code** as the primary development environment for building, training, and validating AISG-aligned AI models
* **Power BI and Tableau** for visualizing risk indicators, trends, and model outputs through interactive dashboards
* **Splunk** for operational monitoring, auditability, and detection of anomalous data access or system behavior

Together, AISG frameworks and these tools enable risk analysts to apply AI responsibly to sensitive retrospective data, supporting evidence-based infant risk analysis while maintaining strong governance and regulatory alignment.

### What are the cases of AI in risk management?

Artificial intelligence (AI) uses retrospective health data to develop and train predictive models for identifying risks and adverse outcomes in babies, both pre-birth and post-birth.

By applying **predictive analytics and machine learning techniques**, these models analyze large datasets — such as:

* Electronic health records (EHRs)
* Medical imaging
* Genetic information
* Inter-Pregnancy Interval (IPI) and Inter-Birth Interval (IBI) data

This analysis detects patterns and individual risk factors associated with sub-optimal intervals and other clinical conditions. This enables:

* Earlier risk detection
* Improved diagnostic accuracy
* More personalized clinical decision-making and interventions

## 11. Data Protection for Babies

### How to protect our children from AI?

Protecting a baby's sensitive retrospective data — such as historical records used for risk certifications — requires a **dual-layered defense** that combines traditional cybersecurity with advanced AI-powered systems.

### Comprehensive Protection Approach

This approach safeguards "babies' data" through:

* **Real-time behavioral analysis** to detect suspicious access patterns
* **Cryptographic proof of authenticity** to ensure data integrity
* **Autonomous defense mechanisms** specifically designed to counter malicious AI capable of weaponizing children's photos and voices for harmful deepfake creation

### Key Security Measures

1. **Access Control**
   * Strict authentication and authorization protocols
   * Role-based access control (RBAC)
   * Multi-factor authentication (MFA)

2. **Data Encryption**
   * Encryption at rest and in transit
   * Secure key management
   * End-to-end encryption for sensitive communications

3. **Audit and Monitoring**
   * Continuous monitoring using platforms like Splunk
   * Comprehensive audit trails
   * Anomaly detection and alerting

4. **Governance Framework**
   * Compliance with data protection regulations
   * Ethical AI guidelines
   * Regular security assessments and penetration testing

5. **AI-Powered Defense**
   * Behavioral analytics to detect unusual data access
   * Machine learning-based threat detection
   * Automated response to security incidents
